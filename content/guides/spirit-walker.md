  - title: "Intro"
    content: |
      The Spirit Walker is one of the coolest Werewolf builds I've put together so far. There's a lot happening on screen, and the build is a lot of fun to play. You're still doing the usual Werewolf things with **Lunar Blessing**, **Lunar Assault**, **Shred**, and **Cross Slash**, but the Spirit Walker adds another layer through its spirits and companions.

      The main idea behind this build is to redirect damage to your companions. That gives us a way to solve one of the biggest problems Werewolf builds have: surviving the hits that get through our defenses. Once that is taken care of, we can use the passive tree to scale both our own damage and the damage of our companions without having to play a full minion build.

  - title: "Skills"
    content: |
      The build uses the usual Werewolf skills, but the most important setup is the one that turns your companions into another layer of defense. Your other skills help you keep damage flowing while the Spirit Walker's spirits and summoned companions do their part.

      ### **Shred**, **Lunar Assault**, and **Cross Slash**

      These are your main Werewolf attacks. **Lunar Blessing** gives you the white Werewolf form and its moonbeams, **Lunar Assault** helps Freeze enemies, **Shred** delivers your damage, and **Cross Slash** finishes enemies off.

      The build's damage comes from executing that rotation while taking advantage of your mobility. You still need to get close to enemies to deal damage, so the defensive setup is especially important.

      ### **Spirit Vessel** and **Lunar Blessing**

      **Forgotten Warden** grants **Spirit Vessel**, a meta skill that lets you socket shapeshifting skills and summon a companion that uses copies of those skills. For this build, the skill you want to socket is **Lunar Blessing**.

      Your **Spirit Vessel** companion transforms into a Werewolf and uses **Lunar Blessing**, bringing the moonbeams and attacks along with it. This adds a substantial amount of damage while giving you another companion that can take redirected damage.

      You can support the **Spirit Vessel** companion with **Loyalty** to redirect more damage to it. **Hulking Minions** is another useful support because it increases the companion's maximum Life without requiring the additional Spirit reservation in this setup. The same support can also benefit the bear from **Wild Protector**.

      Don't worry if **Spirit Vessel**'s tooltip says you cannot have another companion. The companion is still there, as you can see in the build.

      ### **Living Lightning** and the Stag

      The Spirit Walker's spirits add damage on top of your Werewolf attacks. Taking **Sacred Unity** upgrades all three spirits, including the bear's defensive effect and the stags that leap toward enemies.

      The build also uses **Living Lightning** to summon additional minions. These help with **Pack Encouragement**, while the stags add their own damage and Shock. The idea is to get the benefits of having companions around without giving up the Werewolf as your main source of damage.

      ### **Overcharge** and Convergence

      There's a useful interaction between **Overcharge** and Convergence in the **Living Lightning** setup.

      **Overcharge** causes Shock inflicted by supported skills to be reflected back to you, but the resulting Shock has greater magnitude. Convergence removes the ailment from you when you inflict an ailment with a supported skill, and supported skills have greater ailment magnitude when you are afflicted by that ailment at the time.

      **Living Lightning** hits multiple times as it jumps between enemies. That gives the setup repeated opportunities to inflict the stronger Shock and remove the Shock from you on a subsequent hit. It's a neat way to take advantage of **Overcharge** without having to remain Shocked.

      ### **Wind Dancer** and **Savage Fury**

      **Wind Dancer** is an important defensive addition. I wanted to keep **Savage Fury** as well, since it provides a damage buff and Onslaught, so I used an **Unset Ring** to make room for **Wind Dancer** without dropping **Savage Fury**.

      You can drop **Savage Fury** if you prefer, or rearrange your Spirit reservation and skill setup to fit another companion. A companion supported by **Loyalty** can redirect even more damage, but I found that approach more cumbersome and less necessary with the setup shown here.
  - title: "Gear"
    content: |
      The equipment is built around three priorities: increasing the amount of damage your companions can take, improving your Deflection, and scaling your own damage through the passive tree.

      ### Body Armour: Forgotten Warden

      **Forgotten Warden** is the centerpiece of the defensive setup. It provides both Evasion and Energy Shield, grants Deflection Rating based on missing Energy Shield, increases companion maximum Life, and redirects part of the damage from Deflected Hits to a damageable companion before you take it.

      The increased companion Life is particularly important. Every time damage is redirected to a companion, that companion needs to survive. If it dies, you lose the benefit of having it absorb damage.

      The Evasion and Energy Shield base is another reason to use this armour. The build benefits from both defenses, and the additional Deflection Rating from missing Energy Shield makes the hybrid setup especially attractive.

      Forgotten Warden also grants **Spirit Vessel**, which is why the item ties the defensive and offensive sides of the build together.

      ### Helmet: Starkonja's Head

      **Starkonja's Head** adds another layer of damage redirection. It causes 15% of damage from hits to be taken from your damageable companion's Life before you.

      This works alongside Forgotten Warden and the passive tree. The more sources of damage redirection you combine, the less damage you have to take directly. It also makes the companion's maximum Life more valuable, so don't neglect companion survivability when upgrading the build.

      ### Companion Supports

      **Loyalty** helps redirect more damage to supported companions. Combined with Forgotten Warden, Starkonja's Head, and **Captivating Companionship** on the passive tree, it gives you several layers of damage redirection.

      The goal is to make the companion absorb as much damage as possible while keeping it alive. This is why the build invests in companion Life rather than treating the companions as disposable damage sources.

      ### Rings

      One of your rings needs to be an **Unset Ring**. This gives you the extra skill slot needed to run **Wind Dancer** alongside **Savage Fury**.

      The other ring can be chosen around your gear needs. If you want to experiment with an additional companion, you can rearrange your skills and Spirit reservation, but I prefer the simpler setup shown in the build.

      ### Golden Charm: Rite of Passage

      A **Rite of Passage** Golden Charm with the Spirit of the Bear is a strong defensive upgrade. It grants increased maximum Life and reduced damage taken, improving the size of the hits you can survive.

      It's not required for the build to work, but it's a relatively accessible way to make the character tankier. If you're looking for another defensive upgrade after getting the core setup running, this is worth considering.
  - title: "Passive Tree"
    content: |
      The passive tree is where the build brings everything together. We use Deflection to reduce incoming attack damage, redirect part of the damage that gets through to our companions, and scale companion Life so they can keep doing their job.

      ### Deflection and Damage Redirection

      Evasion is a good starting point for the Spirit Walker, but it isn't enough by itself. You cannot evade every attack, and eventually a hit will get through. If that hit is large enough, it can kill you.

      Deflection helps by reducing the damage from attacks that you Deflect. You can invest heavily in Deflection Chance, but even a very high chance doesn't eliminate the possibility of taking a full hit. We still need to increase the size of the hits we can survive.

      That's where the companion setup comes in.

      **Captivating Companionship** causes 5% of damage from hits to be taken from your damageable companions' Life before you. Combined with Starkonja's Head, **Loyalty**, and Forgotten Warden, this gives you multiple sources of damage redirection. When you Deflect a hit, Forgotten Warden adds another layer by redirecting part of that Deflected Hit to a companion.

      The result is a substantial reduction in the damage you take directly. Your companions become part of your defensive setup rather than simply providing extra damage.

      ### Companion Life

      Because companions are absorbing damage, you want them to have as much Life as possible. **Forgotten Warden** already grants increased maximum Life to companions, and **Hulking Minions** provides another way to improve their survivability.

      The bear from **Wild Protector** is particularly useful because **Sacred Unity** grants it **Embrace of the Wild**. While you're in its Presence, you gain Life Regeneration based on your maximum Life, and part of the damage you would take is instead taken by the bear.

      This gives you another defensive layer on top of Deflection and the damage redirection from your gear and passive tree. Keeping the bear alive is important, since it is doing more than just dealing damage.

      ### **Sacred Unity** and the Three Spirits

      **Sacred Unity** is the key Spirit Walker ascendancy notable for this build. To unlock it, you need to allocate **Wild Protector**, **Primal Bounty**, and **Vivid Stampede**.

      **Wild Protector** gives you the bear, which provides the defensive benefit through **Embrace of the Wild**. **Primal Bounty** is based on empowering projectile skills, so its direct offensive value is limited for this build. However, taking it unlocks **Sacred Unity**, making it worthwhile even though we aren't building around projectiles.

      **Vivid Stampede** gives you the stags that leap toward enemies. The stags add damage and Shock, and **The Mórrigan's Guidance** can help sustain the Vivid Wisp cycle by granting a Wisp when **Vivid Stampede** ends. The other small benefit on the way to **Sacred Unity** also gives Critical Hit Chance, so the points aren't wasted.

      You need all three spirits to unlock **Sacred Unity**, but the payoff is worth it. You get the bear's defensive support, the stags' improved targeting, and the ground effect associated with Owl-empowered projectile skills.

      ### **Inspiring Ally** and Companion Damage

      **Inspiring Ally** is one of the main reasons we can scale damage without turning the build into a minion build. It makes increases and reductions to companion damage also apply to you.

      That means passive nodes that grant increased companion damage can improve your own damage at the same time. For example, a node that grants 12% increased companion damage and 10% increased damage while your companion is in your Presence effectively gives you both benefits.

      The tree has several of these nodes, and the bonuses add up quickly. Take advantage of them, but keep the focus on your Werewolf attacks rather than investing so heavily into companions that they become the main build.

      ### **Pack Encouragement**

      **Pack Encouragement** grants increased Attack Damage for each minion in your Presence, up to a maximum of 80%.

      This build has several ways to keep minions around. You have the bear from **Wild Protector**, the **Spirit Vessel** companion from Forgotten Warden, the wolves summoned by **Pounce**, and additional minions from **Living Lightning**.

      Together, these make **Pack Encouragement** a significant source of damage. It also works well with the companion damage bonuses from **Inspiring Ally**, letting you scale your own attacks while keeping the supporting minions useful.

      ### Critical Damage and Other Offense

      Once the defensive setup is in place, invest in Critical Damage Bonus and other offensive modifiers. The build already gets a substantial amount of damage from the companion interactions and **Pack Encouragement**, so additional critical scaling helps push it further.

      **From Nothing** is useful for reaching the area around **Pain Attunement** and nearby passive options without spending points on unnecessary pathing. From there, you can pick up the companion damage nodes and other offensive bonuses that benefit the build.

      You can also invest in Rage and the other damage modifiers available to your tree and gear. The important thing is to get the defensive foundation working first, then add damage without sacrificing the interactions that make the build survive.
  - title: "High-End Upgrades"
    content: |
      The full build is still fairly budget-friendly, but there's room to invest further.

      **Rite of Passage** with the Spirit of the Bell is a good defensive upgrade. Better gear and more investment in companion Life can also make the damage redirection setup more reliable.

      You can experiment with adding another companion and supporting it with **Loyalty**. This can redirect more damage and provide additional offensive benefits, but it requires rearranging your Spirit reservation and skills. I prefer the current setup because it already has plenty of companion support without making the build unnecessarily complicated.

      For a more expensive version, you can explore a low-life setup and other high-end damage options. Those are ways to push the build further, not requirements for getting it running.
  - title: "Final Thoughts"
    content: |
      The Spirit Walker solves a major Werewolf problem by using companions to absorb damage that would otherwise hit you. Forgotten Warden, Starkonja's Head, **Loyalty**, **Captivating Companionship**, and **Sacred Unity** all contribute to that defensive setup.

      Once you've got that working, **Inspiring Ally** and **Pack Encouragement** let you turn the same companion investment into more damage for your Werewolf. The result is a fast, visually chaotic build that remains focused on attacking as a Werewolf rather than playing a dedicated minion build.

      Start with the core defensive interactions, keep your companions alive, and then scale your damage. There's plenty of room to invest further, but the build already has a strong identity without requiring mirror-tier equipment.
